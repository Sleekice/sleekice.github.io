Professional Bio

Artifact 03
Communicating for Learning — Explaining Neural Networks Simply
AIML-500 · Machine Learning Fundamentals  ·  Workshop Three
Title
Neural Networks, Explained Simply — An Exercise in Communication for Learning
Introduction
Albert Einstein is credited with saying that if you can't explain something simply, you don't understand it well enough. This artifact took that challenge head-on: partnering with a generative AI model to craft the clearest possible plain-language explanation of neural networks, then refining it using evidence-based communication and learning strategies from change leadership research.
Description
An audience-friendly written explanation of neural networks developed for the Workshop Three "Communicating for Learning" discussion. The piece explains how brain-inspired networks of neurons learn from examples through weighted connections and gradient descent, distinguishes deep learning from traditional machine learning (automatic feature extraction, many-layered networks), introduces the major architectures — CNNs for images, RNNs for sequential data, and GANs for generating synthetic data — and covers practical concepts like overfitting and the role of GPUs in making deep learning powerful over the last 10–15 years.
Workshop Three Components
This artifact grew out of the full arc of Workshop Three, which had five distinct parts:
  • 3.1 — Adapting to Challenges: a reflective devotional on adaptive leadership, drawing on Moses (Exodus 3:11–12) and Esther (Esther 4:14) as models of courage, wisdom, and strategic thinking under pressure
  • 3.2 — Deep Learning & Neural Networks Quiz: assessed core concepts including ANNs, CNNs, RNNs, GANs, gradient descent, overfitting, and GPU acceleration
  • 3.3 — Questions about Training Methods: an interactive dialogue with an AI chatbot coach exploring supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning
  • 3.4 — Communicating for Learning (this artifact): applying communication and learning-science strategies to explain neural networks simply, with AI as a collaborative tool
  • 3.5 — Portfolio Artifact 3: curating and adapting this work for a professional audience
Objective
To demonstrate two competencies at once: genuine technical understanding of deep learning fundamentals, and the ability to translate that understanding for non-technical audiences — a skill critical for change leaders guiding organizations through AI/ML adoption.
Process
  • Studied the workshop lesson on communication strategies and information presentation for learning
  • Completed the deep learning and neural networks lesson and quiz to solidify the underlying concepts
  • Prompted a generative AI model (Anthropic's Claude) for an analogy-based first draft, disclosing the tool per assignment requirements
  • Refined the explanation by deliberately applying learning-science strategies: chunking, storytelling (a child learning to recognize a dog), relevance and context, and progressive disclosure from foundations to advanced ideas
  • Adapted the discussion post into this portfolio artifact for a broader professional audience
